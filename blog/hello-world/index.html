hello world

hollow Nov 23, 2026 #blog

An introduction, if you will.

Hi there, my name is Hollow. I am 23 years old, and i’m an aspiring security researcher currently learning reverse engineering and binary exploitation.

Hacking mindset

I’m very curious when it comes to cybersecurity, especially finding the interesting vulnerabilities that no one else seems to spot.

An example would be something like a simple username → phone number primitive on the web. I’ll admit this isn’t very useful for binary exploitation, but in reality a strong OSINT foundation is the stepping stone to actually using your exploits.

Think of it like this: you gather your primitives and 0days, and now you have a real-life sudo. Combine an iPhone 0day with a primitive that leaks a phone number from some random app your target is registered on.

More on me

Most of my previous experience has been in web and OSINT, but as I started diving deeper into things like iOS and Android apps, I kept hitting a wall. I had to start learning reverse engineering and modern exploitation, so I sat myself down and realized I needed to go back to the basics and learn again.

Right now I’m working through pwn.college, which I highly recommend to anyone who’s curious about getting into hacking.

Alongside that, I’m enrolled in college for Electrical Engineering. I’m really curious about math and physics, though I’ll admit I need to study more; my math level is honestly just basic algebra right now. I’m only a freshman, just starting out.

I’m honestly really glad to have the passion to start learning again, since I went through a rough patch for a few years. I do tend to struggle with things like my mental health.

Some advice

For anyone just starting out, or anyone who’s lost their passion for hacking, I want to say: hacking is a lifestyle. You can’t do everything in life. I aspire, and have aspired, to do many things, but I still came back to myself.

For years I constantly asked myself: what do I do with my life? Do I do this? Do I study that? I wanted to choose everything. That’s actually part of why I chose Electrical Engineering over computer science. Computer science is something you can learn by sitting down with a computer and enough curiosity, given time. But the hard stuff, like math and modern hacking, comes with time and patience.

To sum it up: sit down with yourself and ask what you’d be willing to sacrifice all your days to. For me, that answer was hacking.

It wasn’t always hacking, though. You tend to hit walls learning this stuff, because it’s genuinely difficult. First I chose guitar, which I spent years learning. Then I told myself I’d go study Japanese abroad, and it didn’t work out, because it wasn’t what I actually wanted. What I want is to sit down and hack things every single day.

Hack the planet.
